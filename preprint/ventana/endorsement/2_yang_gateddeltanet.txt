The paper measures something about the short convolution that your architecture uses. In Gated DeltaNet, and in the Qwen3Next configuration that adopts it, linear_conv_kernel_dim defaults to 4, so the query at a given layer sees the current token and three predecessors. My paper measures what that reach costs when part of a question falls outside it.

The result is that retrieval sensitivity to the excluded component is not small, it is zero to floating point precision, and the model then answers confidently from the part it can still see. I verify this in mamba-130m and mamba-370m, where the layer output moves at every distance while the conv1d output stays at exactly zero. I also report a pre-registered negative alongside it, which is that the effect does not become a behavioural failure once the model has 24 layers to pay with.

Your work is cited as the case where kernel size 4 is the documented default in a deployed architecture, which is what makes the measurement relevant beyond a toy model.
